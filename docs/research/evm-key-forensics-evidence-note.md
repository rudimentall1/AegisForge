# EVM key-generation forensics: evidence note

**Status:** source-supported hypothesis; historical exploit reproduction is still pending.  
**Scope:** static review of public historical source and published incident analysis only. No wallet addresses, private keys, seed phrases, or signing operations are used.

## Evidence collected

1. **Pre-disclosure maintainer issue.** On 2022-01-17, issue [johguse/profanity#61](https://github.com/johguse/profanity/issues/61) asked whether initializing `std::mt19937_64` with `rd()` left the generator effectively initialized by an `unsigned int`, and noted the resulting concern about a small seed space. This is an early, contemporaneous warning—not proof that a particular wallet was generated by this code.
2. **Pinned source snapshot.** The research pipeline pins the pre-disclosure `v1.50` revision `0b54ffc86aafbb206675b4bfecb2ea09ac7a79f6` and archived `master` snapshot `186b9b27ab3e24a1899f648adcabcbf0e5f3131e`. In `Dispatcher.cpp`, `Dispatcher::Device::createSeed()` constructs `std::random_device rd`, initializes `std::mt19937_64 eng(rd())`, then draws four `cl_ulong` values from that engine. The source is fetched and fingerprinted, not compiled or executed.
3. **Published disclosure.** The [1inch report](https://1inch.com/blog/post/a-vulnerability-disclosed-in-profanity-an-ethereum-vanity-address-tool) dated 2022-09-15 describes a 32-bit initial seed expanded deterministically into a large sequence of candidate private keys, and reports a proof of concept that reversed the vanity-address derivation. This supports the research hypothesis, but does not replace an independent reproduction.
4. **Patch context.** A published [advisory diff](https://github.com/manifoldfinance/defi-threat/security/advisories/GHSA-wwj2-6jxj-3333) shows a change from one engine seeded with `rd()` to repeated `rd()` calls to assemble the seed words. This is corroborating historical context; it is not, by itself, proof that a specific later release is safe.

## What the evidence supports

- The historical source contains a potentially weak seed-initialization pattern.
- A contemporaneous project issue raised the limited-seed-space concern before the public disclosure.
- The later disclosure reports that the weakness was exploited by reversing the generator's deterministic derivation.

## What remains unproven by this repository

- A reproducible, independently reviewed experiment against the exact pinned historical implementation.
- The exact effective entropy supplied by `std::random_device` on each relevant build platform and toolchain.
- A reliable version boundary for all affected builds.
- Any claim that a specific address or historical address cohort was generated by Profanity or is vulnerable.

## Required next milestone

Build a test-only, offline reproduction harness from an isolated copy of the pinned source and synthetic test vectors. First validate the derivation and seed expansion against independently authored expected vectors; then have the Validator check the source revision, toolchain/platform assumptions, test output, and limitations. Do not include private-key recovery against real addresses, live-wallet targeting, or any ranked list of suspected vulnerable wallets.
